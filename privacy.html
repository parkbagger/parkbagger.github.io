Park Bagger

Privacy policy

Park Bagger lists the parks near places you save and keeps track of the ones you've visited. This policy explains what the app stores, what it sends, and where it goes.

Last updated November 19, 2026

Summary

  • There is no account. Your places, visits and ratings are stored on your iPhone and synced through your own iCloud account.
  • The developer does not run any servers and does not receive your data.
  • The app has no analytics, no ads and no tracking.
  • To find parks and draw maps, the app sends map locations to public OpenStreetMap services. It does not send your name or any account details.
  • Treasure, a shared game, is off unless you turn it on. Treasure you bury is shared with other players through Apple's iCloud.

Data stored on your device

The app stores the following on your iPhone only:

  • Your places: the name, icon, location and range you set for each one.
  • Parks found near them: names, locations and sizes from OpenStreetMap.
  • Your bags: the date you visited, your rating, any note you wrote, and whether the visit was confirmed by GPS.
  • Your lists (Visit soon and Consider) and your settings.

Deleting the app removes all of this from the phone. You can export a copy as a file from Settings. The file goes wherever you choose to share or save it.

iCloud

The app keeps a copy of your places, bags, ratings, notes, lists and settings in your iCloud account, using Apple's iCloud key-value storage. That lets a new iPhone, or the app after a reinstall, get them back. Park lists from scans are not synced; the app fetches them again.

This copy is stored by Apple under your Apple Account, under Apple's privacy policy: apple.com/legal/privacy. The developer can't see it. Using Reset all in Settings clears it too.

Treasure (opt in)

Treasure lets you bury a virtual treasure at a spot in a park for other people to find. It is off until you turn it on in the Treasure tab. When it's on:

  • Treasure you bury is saved in Apple's CloudKit public database for this app: the exact spot, the park's name, the message you write, and when you buried it. Anyone with Treasure turned on can find it. Treasure can only be buried inside a park, not at home.
  • To check for treasure near you, the app sends your current location to CloudKit while the Treasure tab is open. It is used for that search only and not saved.
  • When you find treasure, the app records the find in CloudKit, linked to that treasure.
  • Your identity: CloudKit links each record to an anonymous identifier for your Apple Account. Other players never see your name or email, and neither does the developer.
  • Reporting: if a message is offensive, report it. The app hides it, stops showing you that person's treasure, and records the report in CloudKit. It then opens an email to support@parkbagger.app with the treasure's ID, park and message, which you can send or discard. Reported treasure is reviewed and removed if it breaks the rules.
  • Removing your data: use Dig up on any treasure you buried to delete it. Turning Treasure off stops all Treasure network requests.

Apple stores this data under its privacy policy: apple.com/legal/privacy.

Location

The app asks for your location only when you use it: to save where you are as a place, to confirm a visit when you bag a park, and, if you turn on Treasure, to follow your position while the Treasure tab is open. It does not track you in the background.

If you decline, you can still add places by searching an address, and bag parks without GPS confirmation.

Services the app contacts

The app talks directly to these services. None of them is run by the developer. Each handles what it receives under its own policy.

  • Overpass API, to find parks and, when you open a beach's details, what's around that beach. The app sends a place's or beach's coordinates, a range, and the kinds of places to look for. It uses public servers at overpass-api.de, overpass.private.coffee, overpass.kumi.systems and maps.mail.ru.
  • Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org), for search. The app sends the address or park name you type. When you use your current location, it sends those coordinates to look up the street address. Searches for parks include the general area of your saved places, so nearby results come first.
  • OpenStreetMap map tiles (tile.openstreetmap.org), to draw maps. Your phone downloads the map images for the area on screen.
  • cdnjs (cdnjs.cloudflare.com), to load the map library the app uses.
  • Google Maps, only when you tap Go for directions. The app opens a Google Maps link with the park's location, in the Google Maps app or Safari.

As with any internet request, these services can see your device's IP address. OpenStreetMap's policy is at osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy.

What the developer collects

Nothing. The app has no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking SDKs, and it does not contact any server run by the developer. Treasure data lives in Apple's CloudKit, not on a developer server.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13.
